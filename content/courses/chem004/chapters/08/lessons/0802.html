What is Molarity?

Molarity is one of the most commonly used concentration units in chemistry and engineering.

It describes the number of moles of solute dissolved in one liter of solution.

M = (Moles of Solute) ÷ (Liters of Solution)
Unit of Molarity:
mol/L
or simply
M

Understanding Molarity

Molarity Meaning
1.0 M NaCl 1 mole NaCl in 1 L solution
2.0 M HCl 2 moles HCl in 1 L solution
0.50 M KNO₃ 0.50 mole KNO₃ in 1 L solution

Figure 1. Higher molarity means more solute particles per unit volume.

Worked Example 1: Calculating Molarity

A solution contains 2.50 moles of NaCl dissolved to make 0.500 L of solution. Determine the molarity.

Given:

Moles = 2.50 mol
Volume = 0.500 L

Step 1: Use the molarity formula.

M = n/V

Step 2: Substitute values.

M = 2.50 ÷ 0.500

Step 3: Solve.

M = 5.00 M
Answer: 5.00 M

Calculating Moles from Molarity

Sometimes engineers know the concentration and volume but need to determine the amount of solute present.

n = MV

where:

  • n = moles
  • M = molarity
  • V = volume in liters

Worked Example 2

How many moles of HCl are present in 750 mL of 2.0 M HCl?

Step 1: Convert volume to liters.

750 mL = 0.750 L

Step 2: Use the formula.

n = MV

Step 3: Substitute values.

n = (2.0)(0.750)

Step 4: Solve.

n = 1.50 mol
Answer: 1.50 mol

Preparing Solutions

Engineers and laboratory technicians frequently prepare solutions of specified concentration.

The general procedure involves:

  1. Calculate required moles.
  2. Convert moles to mass.
  3. Measure the mass.
  4. Dissolve in solvent.
  5. Adjust to the desired volume.

Figure 2. Accurate solution preparation is essential in engineering and laboratory work.

Worked Example 3: Preparing a Solution

How many grams of NaCl are needed to prepare 500 mL of 1.50 M NaCl solution? Molar Mass NaCl = 58.44 g/mol

Step 1: Convert volume to liters.

500 mL = 0.500 L

Step 2: Calculate moles.

n = MV
n = (1.50)(0.500)
n = 0.750 mol

Step 3: Convert moles to mass.

Mass = n × Molar Mass
Mass = (0.750)(58.44)
Mass = 43.83 g
Answer: 43.83 g NaCl

What is Dilution?

Dilution is the process of reducing concentration by adding solvent.

The amount of solute remains constant, but the volume increases.

Figure 3. Dilution decreases concentration by increasing solution volume.

Dilution Equation

Since the number of moles remains constant during dilution:

M1V1 = M2V2

where:

  • M₁ = initial concentration
  • V₁ = initial volume
  • M₂ = final concentration
  • V₂ = final volume

Worked Example 4: Dilution Calculation

A laboratory has 1.50 M HCl. How many milliliters are needed to prepare 500 mL of 0.300 M HCl?

Given:

M₁ = 1.50 M
M₂ = 0.300 M
V₂ = 500 mL

Step 1: Use the dilution equation.

M₁V₁ = M₂V₂

Step 2: Substitute values.

(1.50)V₁ = (0.300)(500)

Step 3: Solve.

V₁ = 100 mL
Answer: 100 mL of stock solution is required.

Check Your Understanding

1. Calculate the molarity of a solution containing 3.0 mol solute in 1.5 L solution.

Given:

n = 3.0 mol
V = 1.5 L

Step 1:

M = n/V

Step 2:

M = 3.0 ÷ 1.5

Step 3:

M = 2.0 M

Answer: 2.0 M

2. Why is molarity widely used in chemistry and engineering?

Molarity directly relates the amount of solute to solution volume, making calculations and solution preparation easier.

3. How many moles are present in 2.0 L of 0.50 M solution?

n = MV
n = (0.50)(2.0)
n = 1.0 mol

Answer: 1.0 mol

4. What happens during dilution?

The concentration decreases because solvent is added while the amount of solute remains constant.

5. Calculate the volume of 2.0 M stock solution needed to prepare 250 mL of 0.50 M solution.

Given:

M₁ = 2.0 M
M₂ = 0.50 M
V₂ = 250 mL

Step 1:

M₁V₁ = M₂V₂

Step 2:

(2.0)V₁ = (0.50)(250)

Step 3:

V₁ = 62.5 mL

Answer: 62.5 mL

6. Why are accurate solution concentrations important in pharmaceutical manufacturing?

Incorrect concentrations can make medications ineffective or unsafe.

7. Calculate the moles present in 750 mL of 1.20 M solution.

Step 1: Convert volume.

750 mL = 0.750 L

Step 2:

n = MV
n = (1.20)(0.750)
n = 0.900 mol

Answer: 0.900 mol

8. Why are dilution calculations important in environmental laboratories?

They allow preparation of calibration standards and accurate testing solutions for environmental analysis.

Lesson Summary

  • Molarity is the number of moles of solute per liter of solution.

  • Dilution decreases concentration by increasing volume.
  • The dilution equation is M₁V₁ = M₂V₂.
  • Engineers use solution chemistry in water treatment, pharmaceutical production, semiconductor manufacturing, and environmental analysis.
  • Accurate solution preparation is essential for safety, quality control, and process reliability.
