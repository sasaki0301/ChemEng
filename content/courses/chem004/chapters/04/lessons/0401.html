Chapter 4: Chemical Nomenclature and Reactions

Lesson 1: Nomenclature of Inorganic Compounds

Engineering Scenario

Imagine a chemical engineer receiving an order for sodium hydroxide, calcium carbonate, sulfuric acid, and ammonium nitrate. If these chemicals were identified only by common names or trade names, mistakes could occur that may lead to equipment damage, safety hazards, or costly production errors. To avoid confusion, scientists and engineers use a standardized naming system called chemical nomenclature. Chemical nomenclature allows professionals worldwide to identify compounds accurately regardless of language or industry.

Learning Objectives

After completing this lesson, you should be able to:
  • Explain the importance of chemical nomenclature.
  • Differentiate chemical formulas from chemical names.
  • Name binary ionic compounds.
  • Recognize and use common polyatomic ions.
  • Name compounds containing variable-charge metals.
  • Name covalent compounds using prefixes.
  • Name common acids.
  • Write chemical formulas from compound names.

Why Chemical Nomenclature Matters

A single chemical may have several common names.

Common Name Chemical Name Formula
Table Salt Sodium Chloride NaCl
Baking Soda Sodium Bicarbonate NaHCO₃
Rust Iron(III) Oxide Fe₂O₃

Without a standardized naming system, communication between scientists, engineers, manufacturers, and healthcare professionals would be extremely difficult.

Chemical Formulas and Chemical Names

A chemical formula tells us:

  • Which elements are present
  • The ratio of atoms present
  • The composition of a compound
Formula Name
H₂O Water
CO₂ Carbon Dioxide
NaCl Sodium Chloride
CaCO₃ Calcium Carbonate
A chemical formula is the symbolic representation of a compound, while a chemical name is its standardized verbal description.

Binary Ionic Compounds

Binary ionic compounds contain:

  • One metal
  • One nonmetal

Naming Rules

  1. Name the metal first.
  2. Name the nonmetal second.
  3. Change the ending of the nonmetal to -ide.
Formula Name
NaCl Sodium Chloride
MgO Magnesium Oxide
CaS Calcium Sulfide
AlN Aluminum Nitride

Worked Example 1

Problem

Name the compound NaBr.

Solution

Na = Sodium (metal)

Br = Bromine → Bromide

NaBr = Sodium Bromide
Answer:
Sodium Bromide

Worked Example 2

Problem

Name MgF₂.

Solution

Mg = Magnesium

F = Fluorine → Fluoride

MgF₂ = Magnesium Fluoride
Answer:
Magnesium Fluoride

Polyatomic Ions

A polyatomic ion is a group of covalently bonded atoms that carries an overall charge.

Unlike single-atom ions, polyatomic ions behave as a single unit during chemical reactions and compound formation.

Figure 2. Polyatomic ions act as single charged units within compounds.

Important Polyatomic Ions

Ion Name Formula Charge
Ammonium NH₄⁺ +1
Hydroxide OH⁻ -1
Nitrate NO₃⁻ -1
Nitrite NO₂⁻ -1
Sulfate SO₄²⁻ -2
Sulfite SO₃²⁻ -2
Carbonate CO₃²⁻ -2

Students often struggle with nomenclature because they do not memorize common polyatomic ions. Familiarity with these ions greatly simplifies chemical naming.

Naming Compounds Containing Polyatomic Ions

The name of the polyatomic ion remains unchanged.

Formula Name
NaOH Sodium Hydroxide
KNO₃ Potassium Nitrate
CaCO₃ Calcium Carbonate
NH₄Cl Ammonium Chloride

Worked Example 3

Problem

Name Ca(NO₃)₂.

Solution

Ca = Calcium

NO₃⁻ = Nitrate

Ca(NO₃)₂ = Calcium Nitrate
Answer:
Calcium Nitrate

Variable-Charge Metals (Stock System)

Some metals can form more than one ion.

Their charge must be specified using Roman numerals.

Formula Name
FeO Iron(II) Oxide
Fe₂O₃ Iron(III) Oxide
CuCl Copper(I) Chloride
CuCl₂ Copper(II) Chloride

Figure 3. Roman numerals indicate the oxidation state of metals with multiple possible charges.

Worked Example 4

Problem

Name Fe₂O₃.

Solution

Oxygen carries a charge of -2.

Three oxygen atoms contribute:

3 × (-2) = -6

The two iron atoms must contribute +6.

+6 ÷ 2 = +3

Each iron ion is Fe³⁺.

Iron(III) Oxide
Answer:
Iron(III) Oxide

Writing Chemical Formulas from Names

To write formulas, determine the ion charges and balance them to produce a neutral compound.

Worked Example 6

Problem

Write the formula for Sodium Sulfate.

Solution

Sodium = Na⁺

Sulfate = SO₄²⁻

Two sodium ions are required.

Na₂SO₄
Answer:
Na₂SO₄

Worked Example 7

Problem

Write the formula for Iron(III) Oxide.

Solution

Fe³⁺ and O²⁻

Balance charges:

Fe₂O₃
Answer:
Fe₂O₃

Engineering Applications

  • Sodium hydroxide is used in water treatment.
  • Calcium carbonate is a major component of cement and concrete.
  • Sulfuric acid is used in lead-acid batteries.
  • Ammonium nitrate is used in fertilizers.
  • Iron oxides are important in corrosion engineering.

4. Why are Roman numerals used in some compound names?

Lesson Summary

  • Chemical nomenclature provides a standardized system for naming compounds.
  • Binary ionic compounds consist of metals and nonmetals.
  • Polyatomic ions behave as charged units and retain their names in compounds.
  • Variable-charge metals require Roman numerals.
  • Covalent compounds use numerical prefixes.
  • Acids follow special naming rules.
  • Writing formulas requires balancing ion charges.
  • Proper nomenclature is essential in science, engineering, medicine, and industry.
