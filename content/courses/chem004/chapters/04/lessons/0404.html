Chapter 4 Summary

Chemical Nomenclature and Reactions

Chapter Overview

In this chapter, we learned how chemists and engineers communicate using standardized chemical names and formulas, how chemical reactions are represented using equations, and how reactions can be classified into recognizable patterns. These concepts are fundamental in engineering because every chemical process—from water treatment and energy production to materials manufacturing and environmental protection—depends on accurate chemical identification and reaction analysis.

Lesson 1 Recap: Nomenclature of Inorganic Compounds

Chemical nomenclature provides a standardized system for naming compounds and writing chemical formulas.

A chemical name identifies a compound, while a chemical formula shows the types and numbers of atoms present.

  • Change the nonmetal ending to -ide.

Chemical equations represent chemical reactions using symbols and formulas.

→ Produces

State Symbols

Symbol Meaning

(aq) Aqueous Solution

Law of Conservation of Mass

Mass of Reactants = Mass of Products

Atoms are neither created nor destroyed during a chemical reaction.

Balancing Chemical Equations

  1. Count atoms on both sides.
  2. Adjust coefficients.
  3. Never change subscripts.
  4. Verify all atom counts.
Only coefficients may be changed when balancing equations.

Example

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

Hydrogen atoms and oxygen atoms are equal on both sides of the equation.

Lesson 3 Recap: Types of Chemical Reactions

Chemical reactions can be classified according to common patterns.

Reaction Type General Pattern
Synthesis A + B → AB
Decomposition AB → A + B
Single Replacement A + BC → AC + B
Double Replacement AB + CD → AD + CB
Combustion Fuel + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O
Neutralization Acid + Base → Salt + Water
Precipitation Formation of an insoluble solid

Synthesis Reactions

2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

Multiple reactants combine to form a single product.

Decomposition Reactions

CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂

A compound breaks into simpler substances.

Single Replacement Reactions

Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu

A more reactive element replaces a less reactive element.

Double Replacement Reactions

AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl + NaNO₃

Ions exchange partners.

Combustion Reactions

CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O

Hydrocarbons react with oxygen to release energy.

Neutralization Reactions

HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O

Acids react with bases to produce salt and water.

Precipitation Reactions

AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl(s) + NaNO₃

An insoluble solid forms from aqueous solutions.

Important Naming Rules

  • Metal + Nonmetal → Ionic Compound
  • Nonmetal + Nonmetal → Covalent Compound
  • Variable-charge metals require Roman numerals.
  • Polyatomic ion names remain unchanged.

Acid Naming Rules

  • -ate → -ic acid
  • -ite → -ous acid

Balancing Rules

  • Balance atoms using coefficients.
  • Never change subscripts.
  • Verify all atom counts.

Chapter Review Questions

1. Why is chemical nomenclature important in engineering?

It ensures accurate communication, proper chemical identification, and safe industrial operations.

2. What is the difference between a coefficient and a subscript?

A coefficient indicates the number of molecules or formula units, while a subscript indicates the number of atoms within a compound.

3. Why must chemical equations be balanced?

To satisfy the Law of Conservation of Mass.

4. What reaction type follows the pattern A + BC → AC + B?

Single Replacement Reaction.

5. Which reaction type is commonly used for wastewater pH control?

Neutralization Reactions.
