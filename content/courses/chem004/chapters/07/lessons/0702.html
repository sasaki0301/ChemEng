Case Study 1: Designing a Gas Storage Tank

A chemical plant needs to store nitrogen gas for industrial use. The engineers must determine the minimum tank size required while keeping pressure within safe limits.

Figure 1. Engineers use gas calculations when designing storage systems.

Worked Example 1

A storage system must contain 500 mol of nitrogen gas at 300 K. The maximum allowable pressure is 10.0 atm. What minimum volume should the tank have?

Step 1: Identify the known values.

  • n = 500 mol
  • T = 300 K
  • P = 10.0 atm
  • R = 0.0821 L·atm/mol·K

Step 2: Rearrange the Ideal Gas Law.

V = nRT/P

Step 3: Substitute values.

V = (500)(0.0821)(300) ÷ 10.0

Step 4: Solve.

V = 1231.5 L
Answer: The tank must have a minimum volume of approximately 1232 L.

Engineering Insight

If engineers attempt to use a smaller tank, pressure will increase beyond the design limit, creating potential safety hazards.

Case Study 3: Medical Oxygen Supply

Hospitals must ensure sufficient oxygen is available for patients during emergencies.

Engineers calculate storage requirements to maintain uninterrupted supply.

Figure 3. Medical facilities rely on accurate gas storage calculations.

Worked Example 3

An oxygen cylinder contains 250 mol of O2 at 20°C and occupies 500 L. Determine the pressure inside the cylinder.

Step 1: Convert temperature.

T = 20 + 273.15
T = 293.15 K

Step 2: Rearrange the Ideal Gas Law.

P = nRT/V

Step 3: Substitute values.

P = (250)(0.0821)(293.15) ÷ 500

Step 4: Solve.

P = 12.03 atm
Answer: The oxygen cylinder pressure is approximately 12.0 atm.

Case Study 4: Automotive Airbags

Airbags must inflate extremely quickly during a collision.

Engineers carefully control the amount of gas generated and the volume of the airbag.

Figure 4. Airbags use rapidly expanding gases to protect passengers.

Engineering Analysis

Designers must answer several questions:

  • How much gas is required?
  • How quickly must inflation occur?
  • What pressure is needed for protection?
  • What materials can withstand the pressure?
The Ideal Gas Law helps engineers estimate the amount of gas needed to inflate an airbag to a target volume and pressure.

Case Study 5: Data Center Cooling

Modern data centers contain thousands of servers that generate substantial amounts of heat.

Figure 5. Proper airflow design is critical for server reliability.

Engineering Analysis

Cooling systems must:

  • Move large volumes of air.
  • Maintain acceptable operating temperatures.
  • Prevent hot spots.
  • Minimize energy consumption.

Poor airflow design can lead to:

  • Server overheating
  • Unexpected shutdowns
  • Reduced hardware lifespan
  • Higher electricity costs

Figure 6. Pipeline engineers must carefully manage gas pressure and flow.

Gas law calculations help engineers determine compressor placement and operating conditions.

Engineering Problem-Solving Process

  1. Identify known variables.
  2. Select the appropriate gas law.
  3. Convert units when necessary.
  4. Perform calculations.
  5. Evaluate whether results are physically reasonable.
  6. Assess safety implications.
Professional engineers do not stop after obtaining a numerical answer. They evaluate whether the answer makes sense and whether it satisfies safety requirements.

Check Your Understanding

1. A storage tank contains 100 mol of gas at 300 K and 5.0 atm. What volume is required?

Given:

n = 100 mol
T = 300 K
P = 5.0 atm

Step 1: Use the Ideal Gas Law.

V = nRT/P

Step 2: Substitute values.

V = (100)(0.0821)(300) ÷ 5.0

Step 3: Solve.

V = 492.6 L

Answer: 492.6 L

2. Why are pressure relief valves important in gas systems?

Pressure relief valves prevent dangerous overpressure conditions that could damage equipment or cause explosions.

3. Which gas law is most commonly used for industrial gas storage calculations?

The Ideal Gas Law because it accounts for pressure, volume, temperature, and amount of gas simultaneously.

4. Why is airflow management important in data centers?

Proper airflow removes heat, improves equipment reliability, and reduces energy consumption.
