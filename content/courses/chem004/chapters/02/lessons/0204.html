Chapter 2: Atomic Structure and Electronic Configuration

Lesson 4: Electron Configuration

Engineering Scenario

Why does copper conduct electricity so well while glass does not? Why can silicon be used to build computer processors? Why are noble gases chemically stable while sodium reacts violently with water? The answer lies in how electrons are arranged within atoms. Electron configuration describes the distribution of electrons among energy levels and orbitals. Understanding electron configuration allows engineers and scientists to predict chemical behavior, electrical conductivity, bonding, and material properties.

  • Write noble gas shorthand electron configurations.
  • Construct orbital diagrams.
  • Relate electron configuration to engineering applications.

Why Electron Configuration Matters

Electrons determine most of the observable chemical and physical properties of matter.

The arrangement of electrons influences:

  • Chemical bonding
  • Electrical conductivity
  • Magnetism
  • Material strength
  • Chemical reactivity
  • Semiconductor behavior

Figure 1. The operation of modern electronic devices depends on electron behavior.

The properties of an element are largely determined by its electron configuration, especially its outermost electrons.

The Three Rules of Electron Configuration

Electrons fill orbitals according to three important rules.

1. Aufbau Principle

The Aufbau Principle states that electrons occupy the lowest available energy orbitals first.

Think of electrons as filling the lowest floors of a building before moving to higher floors.

Figure 4. Electrons fill lower-energy orbitals before higher-energy orbitals.

Electrons always seek the lowest-energy arrangement possible.

2. Pauli Exclusion Principle

No two electrons in the same atom can have identical sets of four quantum numbers.

This means that an orbital can hold a maximum of two electrons with opposite spins.

Figure 5. Two electrons in the same orbital must have opposite spins.

3. Hund's Rule

When electrons occupy orbitals of equal energy, they fill each orbital singly before pairing.

Figure 6. Electrons occupy empty orbitals before pairing.

Electrons prefer to spread out before pairing up.

The Aufbau Filling Order

Electrons fill orbitals according to increasing energy.

Figure 7. The diagonal rule helps determine electron filling order.

1s → 2s → 2p → 3s → 3p → 4s → 3d → 4p → 5s → 4d → 5p → 6s ...

Writing Electron Configurations

Electron configurations indicate how electrons are distributed among orbitals.

Each orbital notation contains:

  • Energy level number
  • Sublevel letter
  • Superscript indicating number of electrons
Example:
1s²
This means:
  • Energy level = 1
  • s orbital
  • Contains 2 electrons

Worked Example 1: Hydrogen

Problem

Write the electron configuration of Hydrogen (Atomic Number = 1).

Solution

Hydrogen has 1 electron.

The first electron enters the lowest-energy orbital, 1s.

1s¹
Answer:
1s¹

Worked Example 2: Carbon

Problem

Write the electron configuration of Carbon (Atomic Number = 6).

Solution

Carbon has 6 electrons.

1s² 2s² 2p²

Check:

2 + 2 + 2 = 6 electrons
Answer:
1s² 2s² 2p²

Worked Example 3: Sodium

Problem

Write the electron configuration of Sodium (Atomic Number = 11).

Solution

Sodium has 11 electrons.

1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s¹

Verification:

2 + 2 + 6 + 1 = 11
Answer:
1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s¹

Noble Gas Shorthand Notation

Long electron configurations can be abbreviated using noble gases.

Sodium: Full Configuration:
1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s¹
Nearest Noble Gas:
[Ne] 3s¹
Noble gas notation simplifies electron configurations for larger atoms.

Engineering Connection

Electron Configuration and Semiconductor Technology

The semiconductor industry relies heavily on electron configurations.

Silicon has the electron configuration:

1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p²

Its four valence electrons make silicon ideal for building transistors and integrated circuits.

Check Your Understanding

1. What principle states that electrons fill the lowest-energy orbitals first?

The Aufbau Principle.

2. How many electrons can occupy a p subshell?

A p subshell can hold a maximum of 6 electrons.

3. Why are noble gases generally unreactive?

They possess complete valence shells, making them very stable.

Lesson Summary

  • Electron configuration describes the arrangement of electrons in atoms.
  • Electrons fill orbitals according to the Aufbau Principle.
  • The Pauli Exclusion Principle limits each orbital to two electrons with opposite spins.
  • Hund's Rule requires electrons to occupy empty orbitals before pairing.
  • s, p, d, and f orbitals have different capacities.
  • Orbital diagrams visually represent electron arrangements.
  • Noble gas notation provides a simplified way to write electron configurations.
  • Electron configuration explains chemical behavior and many engineering applications.
