Chapter 6: Gases and Their Behavior

Lesson 1: Properties of Gases and Kinetic Molecular Theory

Engineering Scenario

Have you ever wondered why airbags inflate instantly during a collision, why compressed gas cylinders must be handled carefully, or how pneumatic systems power industrial machines? The answer lies in the unique behavior of gases. Unlike solids and liquids, gas particles move freely and occupy any available space. Understanding gas behavior is essential in engineering fields such as chemical processing, HVAC systems, environmental engineering, aerospace engineering, and manufacturing.

Learning Objectives

  • Describe the physical properties of gases.
  • Explain the behavior of gases using the Kinetic Molecular Theory.
  • Differentiate between diffusion and effusion.
  • Relate temperature to molecular motion.
  • Apply gas concepts to engineering situations.

What Makes Gases Different?

Matter exists primarily in three states:

  • Solid
  • Liquid
  • Gas

Gases behave very differently from solids and liquids because the particles are much farther apart and move continuously.

Property Solid Liquid Gas
Shape Fixed Takes container shape Fills entire container

Particle Motion Vibrates Flows Moves freely

Properties of Gases

1. Gases Expand to Fill Their Container

Unlike solids and liquids, gases have no fixed shape or volume.

Gas particles spread out until they occupy all available space.

A small amount of perfume released in one corner of a room eventually spreads throughout the room.

2. Gases are Highly Compressible

Because gas particles are separated by large empty spaces, gases can be compressed significantly.

Compressed air tanks, scuba tanks, and aerosol cans store large amounts of gas in relatively small volumes.

Figure 2. Gas particles can be compressed into smaller volumes.

3. Gases Exert Pressure

Gas particles continuously collide with the walls of their container.

These collisions create pressure.

Pressure = Force ÷ Area

The more frequent and forceful the collisions, the higher the pressure.

The pressure inside a bicycle tire is produced by billions of air molecules colliding with the inner walls of the tire.

Understanding Gas Pressure

Pressure is one of the most important properties of gases.

Diffusion

Diffusion is the spontaneous mixing of gas particles due to their constant random motion.

When perfume is sprayed in a room, the scent eventually spreads throughout the room through diffusion.

Figure 3. Gas molecules spread from regions of high concentration to low concentration.

Effusion

Effusion is the movement of gas particles through a tiny opening.

A helium balloon slowly deflates because helium atoms effuse through microscopic openings in the balloon material.

Figure 4. Gas particles escaping through a tiny opening.

Kinetic Molecular Theory (KMT)

The Kinetic Molecular Theory explains the behavior of gases by describing the motion of their particles.

The Kinetic Molecular Theory is the foundation for understanding gas behavior and gas laws.

Assumptions of the Kinetic Molecular Theory

1. Gases Consist of Tiny Particles

Gas particles are extremely small compared to the distance between them.

2. Particles Move Constantly

Gas particles move in continuous random motion.

3. Collisions are Elastic

Particles do not lose kinetic energy when they collide.

4. No Significant Attractions Exist

Gas particles generally do not attract or repel each other.

5. Average Kinetic Energy Depends on Temperature

As temperature increases, particles move faster.

Figure 5. Gas particles move continuously and collide with one another and container walls.

Temperature and Molecular Motion

Temperature is directly related to the average kinetic energy of gas particles.

Higher Temperature ↓ Faster Particle Motion ↓ More Energetic Collisions ↓ Higher Pressure (if volume remains constant)
When a sealed aerosol can is exposed to heat, the gas particles move faster and the internal pressure increases.

Worked Example 1

A sealed container of gas is heated from 25°C to 100°C. What happens to the average kinetic energy of the gas particles?

According to KMT:

  • Temperature increases.
  • Particle speed increases.
  • Average kinetic energy increases.
Answer: The average kinetic energy increases because temperature and kinetic energy are directly related.

Worked Example 2

A gas sample occupies a large room. Why does it not remain concentrated in one corner?

Gas particles move continuously and randomly.

As a result, diffusion occurs.

Answer: Continuous random motion causes the gas to spread throughout the room.

Check Your Understanding

1. Why are gases highly compressible?

Because large empty spaces exist between gas particles.

2. What causes gas pressure?

Collisions between gas particles and container walls.

3. Explain the difference between diffusion and effusion.

Diffusion is the mixing of gases, while effusion is the movement of gas through a tiny opening.

4. What happens to gas particles when temperature increases?

Their average kinetic energy and speed increase.

5. Why are collisions in KMT described as elastic?

Because no kinetic energy is lost during collisions.

6. Why does a basketball become firmer after being inflated?

More gas particles create more collisions with the inner walls, increasing pressure.

7. Why should gas cylinders not be exposed to excessive heat?

Higher temperatures increase particle motion and pressure inside the cylinder.

8. Which KMT assumption explains why gases fill their containers?

Gas particles move continuously and randomly in all directions.

9. Why do odors spread across a room?

Diffusion caused by random molecular motion.

10. Which gas property allows scuba tanks to store large amounts of air?

High compressibility.

Lesson Summary

  • Gases have no fixed shape or volume.
  • Gases are highly compressible because particles are far apart.
  • Gas pressure results from particle collisions with container walls.
  • Diffusion is the mixing of gases, while effusion is the escape of gas through a tiny opening.
  • The Kinetic Molecular Theory explains gas behavior.
  • Gas particles move continuously and randomly.
  • Temperature is directly related to average kinetic energy.
  • Gas behavior plays a major role in engineering systems such as airbags, HVAC systems, pipelines, and compressed gas storage.
