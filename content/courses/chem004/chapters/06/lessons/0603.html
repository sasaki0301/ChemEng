Chapter 6: Gases and Their Behavior

Lesson 3: Ideal Gas Law and Gas Calculations

Engineering Scenario

A chemical engineer needs to determine how many moles of nitrogen gas are stored inside a pressurized tank. A mechanical engineer must calculate the pressure inside an air compressor. An environmental engineer needs to estimate the volume occupied by pollutants released into the atmosphere. These situations involve gases whose pressure, volume, temperature, and amount may all change simultaneously. To solve these problems, scientists use one of the most important equations in chemistry and engineering: the Ideal Gas Law.

Learning Objectives

  • Understand the Ideal Gas Law.
  • Identify the variables in the Ideal Gas Equation.
  • Perform pressure, volume, temperature, and mole calculations.
  • Convert between common gas units.
  • Apply the Ideal Gas Law to engineering situations.
  • Calculate gas density using the Ideal Gas Equation.

From Gas Laws to the Ideal Gas Law

Previously, we studied Boyle's Law, Charles' Law, and Gay-Lussac's Law separately.

Scientists eventually combined all of these relationships into a single equation capable of describing gas behavior under a wide range of conditions.

PV = nRT
The Ideal Gas Law combines pressure, volume, temperature, and amount of gas into one powerful equation.

Understanding the Variables

Symbol Meaning Typical Units
P Pressure atm, kPa, mmHg

The Gas Constant (R)

The value of R depends on the pressure units being used.

Value of R Pressure Unit
0.0821 L·atm/mol·K atm
8.314 L·kPa/mol·K kPa
62.36 L·mmHg/mol·K mmHg
Throughout this lesson, we will primarily use:
R = 0.0821 L·atm/mol·K

Temperature Must Always Be Kelvin

The Ideal Gas Law requires absolute temperature.

K = °C + 273.15
25°C = ?
25 + 273.15 = 298.15 K
Never substitute Celsius temperatures directly into the Ideal Gas Equation.

Solving for Pressure

Rearrange the Ideal Gas Law:

P = nRT/V

Worked Example 1

Determine the pressure exerted by 2.00 mol of gas occupying 10.0 L at 27°C.

Step 1: Convert temperature.

T = 27 + 273.15
T = 300.15 K

Step 2: Write equation.

P = nRT/V

Step 3: Substitute values.

P = (2.00)(0.0821)(300.15) ÷ 10.0

Step 4: Solve.

P = 4.93 atm
Answer: 4.93 atm

Solving for Volume

V = nRT/P

Worked Example 2

Determine the volume occupied by 3.00 mol of gas at 2.50 atm and 300 K.

Step 1: Write formula.

V = nRT/P

Step 2: Substitute values.

V = (3.00)(0.0821)(300) ÷ 2.50

Step 3: Solve.

V = 29.56 L
Answer: 29.56 L

Solving for Number of Moles

n = PV/RT

Worked Example 3

A gas occupies 12.0 L at 1.50 atm and 298 K. Determine the number of moles present.

Step 1: Write equation.

n = PV/RT

Step 2: Substitute values.

n = (1.50)(12.0) ÷ (0.0821)(298)

Step 3: Solve.

n = 0.736 mol
Answer: 0.736 mol

Solving for Temperature

T = PV/nR

Worked Example 4

A gas occupies 15.0 L at 2.0 atm and contains 1.20 mol. Determine its temperature.

Step 1: Write equation.

T = PV/nR

Step 2: Substitute values.

T = (2.0)(15.0) ÷ (1.20)(0.0821)

Step 3: Solve.

T = 304.5 K
Answer: 304.5 K

Standard Temperature and Pressure (STP)

A standard reference condition is often used when studying gases.

Quantity Value
Pressure 1 atm
Temperature 273.15 K (0°C)
At STP:
1 mol gas = 22.4 L

Worked Example 5

How many liters are occupied by 5.0 mol of an ideal gas at STP?
5.0 mol × 22.4 L/mol
112 L
Answer: 112 L

Gas Density and the Ideal Gas Law

The Ideal Gas Law can also be used to calculate the density of gases.

d = PM/RT

where:

  • d = density
  • P = pressure
  • M = molar mass
  • R = gas constant
  • T = temperature

Worked Example 6

Determine the density of oxygen gas (O2) at 1 atm and 298 K. Molar Mass O2 = 32.00 g/mol

Step 1: Use density equation.

d = PM/RT

Step 2: Substitute values.

d = (1)(32.00) ÷ (0.0821)(298)

Step 3: Solve.

d = 1.31 g/L
Answer: 1.31 g/L

4. Why must Kelvin be used in the Ideal Gas Law?

The Ideal Gas Law requires absolute temperature values. Celsius temperatures would give incorrect results.

n = number of moles.

P = nRT/V
P = (1.0)(0.0821)(300) ÷ 24.6
P = 1.00 atm

7. What is STP?

1.5 × 22.4
33.6 L

9. Which equation is used to calculate gas density?

d = PM/RT

10. Why is the Ideal Gas Law important in engineering?

It allows engineers to predict pressure, volume, temperature, and gas quantities in real systems such as compressors, pipelines, and storage tanks.

Lesson Summary

  • The Ideal Gas Law combines pressure, volume, temperature, and amount of gas into a single equation.
  • The Ideal Gas Equation is PV = nRT.
  • Temperature must always be expressed in Kelvin.
  • The Ideal Gas Law can be rearranged to solve for pressure, volume, moles, or temperature.
  • At STP, one mole of an ideal gas occupies 22.4 L.
  • The equation d = PM/RT can be used to determine gas density.
  • The Ideal Gas Law is widely used in engineering, industrial processes, environmental systems, and gas storage applications.
